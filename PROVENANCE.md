# Source provenance

## Research foundation

[src/reference/sha256.c](src/reference/sha256.c) and [src/reference/sha256.h](src/reference/sha256.h) are unchanged copies of `sha256/c/sha256.c` and `sha256/c/sha256.h` from the original research workspace. Hashprobe uses their standard 64-round `sha256()` entry point. The original sources remain in place.

| File | SHA-256 |
| --- | --- |
| `sha256.c` | `763a98844fc45d8f1cf4f7ee5fe9cd5c1d849d7f778e2d34c154692c4775f4a1` |
| `sha256.h` | `d9ff872c21acc547238c88c53c40e86842c6f89a651e236f817f319aa9b530f4` |

The four known answers were carried over from `sha256/python/test_sha256.py`: empty input, `abc`, the standard 56-byte message, and one million `a` bytes. Boundary and random cases are generated by Hashprobe. This release does not bundle or claim to run the complete NIST CAVP vector collection.

## Included JSON library

The files in [vendor/cjson](vendor/cjson) come from [cJSON v1.7.19](https://github.com/DaveGamble/cJSON/releases/tag/v1.7.19), obtained from the upstream repository. They are unchanged and compiled directly into Hashprobe with a JSON nesting limit of 32. The upstream [MIT license](vendor/cjson/LICENSE) is included and applies to those files.
